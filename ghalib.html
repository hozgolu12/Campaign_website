The agenda

Six commitments. Open to scrutiny.

Each commitment names what will be done and how students can check it. Tap a heading to read it.

Value based representation

A published code of conduct for the campaign and for the term in office, regular open meetings with students, and decisions explained in public.

Engineering and Technology

Better access to labs and workshops, department-wise study material, and support for student projects, hackathons and technical societies.

Student welfare

A simple grievance desk with follow-up on hostel, dining, library and administrative concerns, and a visible status for every complaint.

Academic activism

Study circles, debates, guest talks and student-led seminars that connect classroom learning to the issues students care about.

Careers and skills

Peer-led skill sessions, internship and placement information in one place, and alumni mentoring for final-year students.

Transparency

A public list of promises with progress marked as done, in progress or pending, and a written report back to students each month.

Proposals are subject to feasibility and university approval.

First 100 days

Make progress visible.

The aim is a process students can follow, question and judge — not a promise that every problem disappears overnight.

Days 1–30

Listen.

Meet students department by department, set up one place to send concerns, and publish how they will be handled.

Days 31–70

Prioritise.

Pick the first priorities with students, check the relevant rules, and prepare proposals backed by evidence.

Days 71–100

Report.

Take proposals to the right offices, then publish what moved, what is pending and why.
